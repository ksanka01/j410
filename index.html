Find N/A drinks that excite.

Gen Z are looking to non-alcoholic drinks to satisfy their quench. This website serves as a primer and guide for options in San Luis Obispo.

By Krithi Sankar | May 13, 2026

Welcome to Zero Proof SLO! This homepage is for San Luis Obispo residents looking to explore the variety of non-alcoholic offerings available on the Central Coast.

The wine and beer industry has faced a pivotal moment. Factors like wine grape oversupply, restrictive federal policies and lower demand in younger consumers have contributed to the downfall of beverage sales, according to Silicon Valley Bank’s 2026 Wine Industry Report. N/A drinks now fill in the gap for the amalgamation of changes happening today. <p> and </p> tags.

While premium markets like Napa and Sonoma counties experience most of the impact, San Luis Obispo county is just as vulnerable to these shifts. The county is home to roughly 250 wineries and 30 breweries – the most of any other jurisdiction in California. As a result, the local economy depends on tourism and consistent consumer behavior, both of which took a hit during the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic. With state and local lockdown policies in place, consumers tended to drink less, especially since drinking is often considered a social activity. Another byproduct of the times was an increased awareness of health. Since 2020, 53% of Americans think even moderate drinking (one to two drinks) is bad for their health, according to a recent Gallup poll.

As these habits began to change, so did the industry’s power players. Companies like Athletic Brewing Company began to dominate – and traditional beer companies followed suit. In the last four years, the volume sales of N/A drinks has increased by 111%, while doubling its presence in the industry from 1.1 to 2.5%, according to the Brewers Association.

Terry Lease, a Cal Poly wine business professor, confirmed the profitability of N/A drinks in this ever-changing industry.

“‘Non-alc’ drinks used to be seen as a joke,” Lease said. “Now, there’s so many possibilities that they may overtake wines especially in the next five years.”

Jordan Ziegler, a barrel production manager at Firestone Walker, shared these sentiments, saying customers coming to their brewery are looking for taste, rather than impact of alcohol.

“People don’t want to be buzzed when they go out,” Ziegler said. “We try to keep the same crisp feel of a beer without the bad parts of drinking alcohol.”

Firestone Walker, one of the most well-known breweries on the Central Coast, was originally meant to be a non-alcoholic brewery, according to the Santa Barbara Independent. While they may have released an N/A beer in the 1980’s, Firestone has recently come out with their first non-alcoholic beer in decades: 8ZERO5. I spoke with Ziegler at length about how this brewing process differs for 8ZERO5 in comparison to the production of other drinks.
